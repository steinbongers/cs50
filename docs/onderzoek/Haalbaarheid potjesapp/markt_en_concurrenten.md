# Market and competitors: Dutch "potjes" personal-finance app for 18-30-year-olds

Research date: 9 October 2026. Primary scope: Netherlands; secondary: EU/UK. Source dates are given where known. Several sources are comparison or affiliate sites, which are marked as such.

## 1. What Dutch banks and neobanks already offer young people in their own apps

### Takeaway
Every major Dutch bank app (ING, ABN AMRO, Rabobank) and the neobanks (bunq, Revolut) now offer category budgets, savings pots/goals and spending insights. All of them categorize **automatically**, and they show only that bank's own accounts. None is built around a habit where the user sorts every transaction manually with one tap. bunq comes closest to real "envelopes" because its budgets are actual sub-accounts.

### Cited Findings
**ING**
- ING customers can create several "potjes" (spaardoelen) within an account. In 2025 ING added "Spaarslot", which locks the savings account for a few hours to prevent impulse spending — [iCulture](https://www.iculture.nl/nieuws/ing-app-spaarslot/) (Aug 2025, per search summary)
- In the ING app, customers can set monthly budgets per spending category. During setup the app shows the last three months of spending in that category — [Banken.nl](https://www.banken.nl/nieuws/25618/ing-voegt-budget-optie-toe-aan-mobiele-app); [Credit Expo](https://www.creditexpo.nl/ing-budgetteren-zorgt-voor-meer-grip-op-je-geld/)
- In September 2025 ING ran the "Spaartaxi" campaign aimed at 18-25-year-olds (6 weeks), steering them to spaardoelen and potjes in the ING app — [InFinance](https://infinance.nl/geldzaken/sparen/ing-zet-spaartaxi-in-om-jongeren-te-leren-sparen/); [Van Spaarbank Veranderen](https://www.vanspaarbankveranderen.nl/sparen/actueel/nieuws-en-blog/3710/spaartaxi-spaarrente-voor-gen-z/)
- ING Jongerenrekening (12-17): you can save toward goals, but you cannot pay from separate potjes. A comparison site calls the budgeting "vrij eenvoudig" (fairly basic) — [Finler](https://www.finler.nl/betaalrekening/ing-jongerenrekening/) (comparison site)

**ABN AMRO**
- ABN AMRO announced on 24 Oct 2022 that it would discontinue the standalone budgeting app **Grip**. The app disappeared on 16 Dec 2022, and "relevant functions" moved into the ABN AMRO app. No reason or user count was given — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/abn-stopt-huishoudboekje-grip); [ABN AMRO press release](https://www.abnamro.com/nl/nieuws/integratie-grip-functies-in-abn-amro-app)
- After the shutdown, non-ABN AMRO customers could no longer use the functions. Account data was deleted and could not be exported — [Financer.nl](https://financer.nl/persoonlijke-financien/grip-app-review/) (affiliate site); [ID.nl](https://id.nl/huis-en-entertainment/computer-en-gaming/software/de-beste-apps-om-je-financien-op-orde-te-houden)
- In the ABN AMRO app, budgets sit under "Inzicht". You choose a category, a period and an amount. Budgets can repeat weekly, monthly or yearly, or run between custom dates. Users can change the start date of their "financial month", and the app can cancel subscriptions — [ABN AMRO Budgetten](https://www.abnamro.nl/nl/prive/themas/overzicht-en-inzicht/budgetten.html); [ABN AMRO Inzicht](https://www.abnamro.nl/nl/prive/themas/overzicht-en-inzicht/inzicht-met-de-app.html)
- ABN AMRO owns **Tikkie** (see Q3). The leading Dutch repayment tool is therefore a bank product, but it is separate from budgeting — [ABN AMRO](https://www.abnamro.com/nl/nieuws/tikkie-beleeft-nieuw-recordjaar-en-doorbreekt-grens-van-een-miljard)

**Rabobank**
- Rabo Budgets live under "Inzicht" in the Rabo App. You pick a category and a target amount, then see spending against it. No money is moved: it is an insight tool only. There are also savings pots with a progress bar and a monthly spending forecast — [Rabobank Budgets](https://www.rabobank.nl/particulieren/online-bankieren/inzicht/budgets); [Rabobank Inzicht](https://www.rabobank.nl/particulieren/online-bankieren/inzicht); [Banken.nl](https://www.banken.nl/nieuws/25807/ons-uitgavenpatroon-is-vaak-een-weerspiegeling-van-ons) (2024)

**bunq**
- With "Easy Budgeting", each budget is a dedicated sub-account. You choose a category, amount, budget type and top-up frequency. bunq automatically assigns payments in that category to the matching account and alerts you at 80%, 95% and 100% of the limit — [bunq help: Easy Budgeting](https://help.bunq.com/nl-nl/articles/easy-budgeting); [bunq features](https://www.bunq.com/en-us/personal/features/budgeting); [bunq FAQ](https://nl.bunq.com/faq/can-i-set-up-multiple-sub-accounts-with-easy-budgeting)
- Insights categorizes spending automatically, suggests budgets for the top spending categories and offers a balance forecast. "Income Organizer" splits salary across sub-accounts — [bunq blog](https://www.bunq.com/blog/the-ultimate-guide-to-budgeting-with-bunq); [iCulture](https://www.iculture.nl/nieuws/bunq-insights-uitgaven-categorieen/) (older article)

**Revolut**
- Pockets (the successor to Vaults) are Revolut's budgeting feature for setting money aside per goal. They support one-off or recurring transfers and round-ups, and there are personal and **Group** pockets for shared goals with friends. Pockets is available in NL, and budgeting is included in the free plan — [Revolut help NL: Pockets](https://help.revolut.com/en-NL/help/app-features/vaults/what-are-revolut-vaults/); [Revolut help NL: Group Pocket](https://help.revolut.com/en-NL/help/app-features/vaults/how-do-i-manage-a-group-pocket/); [Revolut NL budget planner](https://www.revolut.com/en-NL/best-budget-planner/)
- Revolut reached 1 million Dutch customers in 2025 and 1.5 million in March 2026, adding about 50,000 a month. About 60% use the account monthly. The target is 2 million by 2027 — [Banken.nl](https://www.banken.nl/nieuws/26926/revolut-groeit-door-naar-15-miljoen-nederlandse-klanten); [NU/nieuws.nl](https://nieuws.nl/economie/onlinebank-revolut-kreeg-het-afgelopen-jaar-de-helft-meer-klanten); [FD](https://fd.nl/financiele-markten/1590263/revolut-boekt-62-hogere-winst-over-2025)

**Other**
- Buut is a youth/family account with spending and savings potjes plus parental oversight, so it sits below the 18+ target group — [Finler](https://www.finler.nl/betaalrekening/buut-rekening/)
- N26 has built-in budgeting, alerts and savings goals with automatic categorization, but requires an N26 account — [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/persberichten/de-10-slimste-budget-apps-van-2025-inzicht-zonder-gedoe/) (sponsored, Aug 2025)

### Inferences
- **Overlap with the planned app:** budget per category (all banks), savings goal per pot (ING, Rabo, Revolut, bunq), and a ring or circle chart of spending (bunq Insights). These are table stakes and will not set the app apart on their own.
- **What the banks lack:** (a) one view across several banks; (b) a deliberate manual sorting ritual (all bank apps auto-categorize, which removes the "awareness" moment); (c) a daily streak or evening reminder; (d) linking "I paid for friends" to getting the money back inside the budget. Revolut Group Pockets and bunq budget sub-accounts are the nearest substitutes for parts of this.
- Many 18-30s hold a bunq or Revolut account next to a big-bank account. Revolut's 1.5M NL customers are skewed toward young people (inferred, not sourced). This multi-bank situation is the strongest argument for a PSD2 aggregator.

### Gaps
- I did not verify ING's current "Inzicht" features in detail, such as whether users can recategorize manually or whether budgets are monthly only. ING's own NL pages did not come up in search.
- I found no sourced information on Knab, ASN, SNS or RegioBank (de Volksbank) youth budgeting features. From background knowledge, Knab was acquired by BAWAG (2024). This is unverified here and needs checking.
- I found no source confirming whether bunq still offers a native split-bill feature, or its current Dutch customer count.

## 2. Independent PFM apps in NL/EU: positioning, categorization, NL bank linking, pricing, status, failures

### Takeaway
The independent Dutch PFM market has thinned out. Yolt closed in 2022, Grip in 2022, and MijnGeldzaken was absorbed into Dyme. Dyme itself was sold to insurer RISK in 2025 and is positioned on subscriptions and savings, not budgeting. The remaining budgeting apps are either automatic and rule-based (Flow, Spendle rules, Emma) or fully manual with no bank link (Monefy, Budgetbeheerder). YNAB is the only well-known envelope/"give every euro a job" tool, and its Dutch bank sync is unreliable and costs about USD 109 per year.

### Cited Findings
**Dutch / NL-active**
- **Dyme** (Amsterdam, founded 2018): a subscription manager and "huishoudboekje". In 2019 it became the first independent Dutch company with a PSD2 licence from DNB — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyme_(company))
  - **Acquired by insurtech RISK** (backed by Söderberg & Partners), announced on 10 Sep 2025. The brand and management stay, and a German rollout is planned for 2026 — [Peak Capital](https://peak.capital/fintech-startup-dyme-acquired-by-risk/); [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/dyme-verkocht-insurtech-risk-miljoenendeal); [Quote](https://www.quotenet.nl/zakelijk/a66036154/dyme-joran-iedema-amsterdam-risk-verzekeringen-fintech-insurtech/)
  - More than 600,000 Dutch people have linked their bank to Dyme since 2018, saving over EUR 40M. Dyme had its first profitable quarter in 2024 — [Peak Capital](https://peak.capital/fintech-startup-dyme-acquired-by-risk/); [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/dyme-acquired-by-risk/)
  - RISK presented the deal as gaining Gen Z customers: "Wij hebben jonge gebruikers, Risk heeft de tools" — [Quote](https://www.quotenet.nl/zakelijk/a66036154/dyme-joran-iedema-amsterdam-risk-verzekeringen-fintech-insurtech/); [AMweb](https://www.amweb.nl/154491/risk-neemt-fintech-app-dyme-over-gen-z-als-nieuwe-doelgroep)
  - The free version has limited budgeting features — [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/persberichten/de-10-slimste-budget-apps-van-2025-inzicht-zonder-gedoe/) (sponsored)
- **MijnGeldzaken.nl** (formerly AFAS Personal): "Mijngeldzaken.nl is overgegaan naar Dyme". Former users report missing features and history — [Spendle](https://www.spendle.me/getting-started/from-mijngeldzaken/) (competitor page); [MijnGeldzaken FAQ](https://www.mijngeldzaken.nl/meestgesteldevragen/mijngeldzaken/huishoudboekje---grip-van-abnamro-stopt-ermee-kan-ik-overstappen)
- **Spendle** (NL): a household-ledger app for net worth, budgets, a forecast and a cash account. It links banks via PSD2 through GoCardless or by CSV import. Categorization is manual for uncategorized items, with rules that the app offers to create after you categorize by hand. There is no potjes/envelope concept. Price: 14-day trial, then **EUR 23 per year** — [Spendle](https://www.spendle.me/getting-started/from-mijngeldzaken/)
- **Flow Your Money** (NL): rule-based automation that distributes money according to user-defined "flows". It claims DNB/AFM supervision. Reviewers say it is *less* suited to people who want to check every transaction themselves — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=money.flowyour.flow&hl=nl) (self-reported); [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/persberichten/de-10-slimste-budget-apps-van-2025-inzicht-zonder-gedoe/); [DeZaak](https://www.dezaak.nl/blog-content/beste-budget-apps-van-2025-welke-helpt-jou-echt-grip-te-krijgen-op-je-geld/)
- **Grassfeld**: a dashboard with bank linking and automatic categorization. **Monefy** and **Budgetbeheerder**: manual entry, no bank linking. **Spendee**: links multiple accounts and offers shared household budgets, with a freemium model — [Computable](https://www.computable.nl/persberichten/de-10-slimste-budget-apps-van-2025-inzicht-zonder-gedoe/) (sponsored, 4 Aug 2025)

**Failed / shut down**
- **Yolt** (ING's consumer PFM/aggregation app): ING closed the consumer app to focus on its B2B arm, Yolt Technology Services, after judging that it had not reached the desired scale. It had about 1.5M users in 2020 (mostly UK) and could not compete with neobanks' built-in PFM. It ceased around 30 Aug 2022, with liquidation announced Sep 2022 — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/ing-says-goodbye-yolt); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/yolt/financials); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/software/yolt/about)
- **ABN AMRO Grip**: closed on 16 Dec 2022 and folded into the bank app (see Q1) — [Emerce](https://www.emerce.nl/nieuws/abn-stopt-huishoudboekje-grip)
- **MijnGeldzaken**: absorbed into Dyme (date not found) — [Spendle](https://www.spendle.me/getting-started/from-mijngeldzaken/)

**International**
- **YNAB**: 34-day trial, then USD 14.99 per month or USD 109 per year, about USD 132 including Dutch VAT by one estimate. It added European bank connections via Plaid in Feb 2025, but publishes no EU bank list ("select European banks") — [Freenance](https://freenance.io/products/ynab-pricing-2026/)
  - Sources disagree on Dutch bank support. Yieldfund says some Dutch banks import automatically via Plaid/Salt Edge, while Expat Netherlands Hub says there is no automatic sync with Dutch banks. Synci says EU coverage is thin, so third-party bridges such as Synci exist — [Yieldfund](https://www.yieldfund.com/news/9-best-budgeting-apps-in-the-netherlands); contradicted by [Expat NL Hub](https://expatnetherlandshub.com/guides/finance/best-finance-apps-expats-netherlands-2026/); [Synci](https://synci.io/destinations/ynab)
- **Emma** (London): Open Banking aggregator with automatic categorization and subscriptions. 2026 tiers are Plus at GBP 4.99 per month, Pro at GBP 9.99 per month and Ultimate at GBP 14.99 per month, with two bank logins free. Dutch bank coverage is not confirmed — [Money to the Masses](https://moneytothemasses.com/quick-savings/tips/the-best-budgeting-apps-in-the-uk-how-to-budget-without-trying); [Finder](https://www.finder.com/uk/budgeting/emma-review)
- **Snoop, Plum, Emma** remain active UK apps in 2026 comparisons. Snoop focuses on bill deals and Plum on automatic saving — [Which?](https://www.which.co.uk/money/banking/banking-security-and-payment-methods/open-banking-budgeting-and-saving-apps-aLl3e0g9I7Ft); [Boring Money](https://www.boringmoney.co.uk/learn/articles/budgeting-apps-reviewed/)
- Open-source/DIY: Dutch users build their own bank importers for Actual Budget, which suggests unmet demand for envelope budgeting with Dutch bank feeds — [Medium](https://rfrancocantero.medium.com/building-custom-bank-import-tools-for-actual-budget-a-dutch-banking-story-bc47429f7ae9)

### Inferences
- **Why the Dutch PFM apps failed or pivoted:** (1) the banks copied the core features (budgets, insights) for free inside the app people already open daily, as Grip was absorbed and Yolt lost to neobank PFM; (2) aggregation alone did not give enough engagement or scale to monetize, as with Yolt; (3) the survivors monetized something other than budgeting, such as Dyme's subscription cancelling and now insurance cross-sell via RISK. A potjes app needs a clear reason to be opened daily that bank apps cannot easily copy. A behavioural ritual (swipe, streak, reminder) plus a social element (getting money back) is more defensible than features.
- **Closest functional competitor:** YNAB, through manual envelope budgeting and approving each imported transaction. Its weaknesses for Dutch 18-30s are price (about EUR 100+ per year), English only, unreliable Dutch bank sync, a steep learning curve and no repayment flow. Spendle (EUR 23 per year, PSD2) is the closest Dutch tool, but it is a household ledger for an older audience with no envelopes or gamification.
- Freemium with a low price (EUR 0-25 per year) seems to be the Dutch market norm. The UK apps' GBP 5-15 per month pricing does not appear in NL.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable data on these apps named in the brief: **Copilot Money** (US/iOS; EU availability not verified), **Finny**, **Floow** (no matches; possibly confused with Flow), **Toshl**, **Goodbudget**, **Wallet by BudgetBakers**, **Frollo** (Australia), or Monefy ratings. I collected no App Store or Google Play ratings or review counts because of the limit on tool calls.
- Dyme's current budgeting feature set after the RISK acquisition, and whether it still offers manual categorization, is not verified.
- I did not verify the exact date MijnGeldzaken moved to Dyme.

## 3. Splitting and repayment tools: size and integration

### Takeaway
Tikkie dominates Dutch repayment: more than 10 million users, over 1 billion payment requests ever and 170M Tikkies in 2025. It is a payment-request tool, not a budget tool. WieBetaaltWat (5M users claimed) and Splitwise/Tricount handle group cost ledgers but do not link to bank transactions. Nobody combines "this bank transaction was partly for friends" with getting the money back and correcting the pot balance.

### Cited Findings
- **Tikkie** (ABN AMRO, launched 2016) passed 10 million users by the end of 2025 and the 1 billion payment requests mark. In 2025 Dutch users sent more than 170 million Tikkies worth EUR 8.5 billion, and there are about 37,000 business users — [ABN AMRO NL](https://www.abnamro.com/nl/nieuws/tikkie-beleeft-nieuw-recordjaar-en-doorbreekt-grens-van-een-miljard); [ABN AMRO EN](https://www.abnamro.com/en/news/tikkie-celebrates-new-record-year-and-surpasses-threshold-of-one-billion); [ICT Magazine](https://www.ictmagazine.nl/nieuws/infographic-miljardste-tikkie-betaald-in-2025/)
- **WieBetaaltWat** (founded 2005, NL) claims 5 million users and offers a Splitwise import — [WieBetaaltWat](https://wiebetaaltwat.nl/splitwise-overstapservice/) (self-reported)
- **Splitwise** does not process payments in NL; it only tracks who owes what. The free version has a daily expense limit, and Pro costs about EUR 2.99 per month or EUR 29.99 per year — [Financer.nl](https://financer.nl/persoonlijke-financien/wie-betaalt-wat/); [App Store NL](https://apps.apple.com/nl/app/splitwise/id458023433)
- **Tricount** (acquired by bunq in 2022, per background knowledge and not sourced here) is free and unlimited, with more than 21M users worldwide according to its App Store listing — [App Store NL](https://apps.apple.com/nl/app/tricount-uitgaven-delen/id349866256)
- **Revolut Group Pockets** support shared money goals with friends — [Revolut help](https://help.revolut.com/en-NL/help/app-features/vaults/how-do-i-manage-a-group-pocket/)

### Inferences
- Copying Tikkie's payment infrastructure is unrealistic. The opportunity is to **integrate around** it. When a user marks a bank transaction as "voorgeschoten", the app records the share owed, creates or links a payment request (for example a Tikkie link or a Wero/iDEAL request) and matches the incoming repayment to the original pot so the pot shows net spending. No found competitor does this net-of-repayment budgeting.
- Tricount, now inside bunq, means bunq may be best placed to copy this integration for its own customers.

### Gaps
- **"Voorgeschoten"** could not be found as an existing app in two searches. It may not exist as a public product, or it may be a name or design reference within this project. This needs verification.
- I did not verify Tikkie's API availability for third parties, or whether a "Wie betaalt wat"-style group feature exists inside Tikkie today.
- No data was found on bunq's own split-bill feature.

## 4. White space: is "one-tap manual sorting of bank-linked transactions + getting money back" distinctive?

### Takeaway
Yes, as a **combination**, though no single element is unique. Banks and most apps compete on *automatic* categorization, while YNAB owns *manual* envelope budgeting but is expensive, English-only, weak on Dutch bank sync and has no repayment flow. A Dutch, PSD2 multi-bank, swipe-to-sort potjes app with a streak or reminder and a built-in "voorgeschoten / get money back" flow has no direct competitor found in NL. The main risks are that bank apps already cover "good enough" budgeting for free, and that the history of Dutch PFM apps (Yolt, Grip, MijnGeldzaken) shows standalone budgeting struggles to monetize.

### Cited Findings
- Bank apps auto-categorize and budget per category for free (ING, ABN AMRO, Rabobank, bunq). Rabo budgets do not move money — [Rabobank](https://www.rabobank.nl/particulieren/online-bankieren/inzicht/budgets); [ABN AMRO](https://www.abnamro.nl/nl/prive/themas/overzicht-en-inzicht/budgetten.html); [bunq](https://help.bunq.com/nl-nl/articles/easy-budgeting)
- Flow is explicitly described as not for people who want to check every transaction themselves, which positions manual review as a separate segment — [DeZaak](https://www.dezaak.nl/blog-content/beste-budget-apps-van-2025-welke-helpt-jou-echt-grip-te-krijgen-op-je-geld/)
- Spendle offers manual assignment plus rules with PSD2, but has no envelopes or gamification — [Spendle](https://www.spendle.me/getting-started/from-mijngeldzaken/)
- YNAB costs USD 109 per year, has uncertain Dutch bank sync and is English-only — [Freenance](https://freenance.io/products/ynab-pricing-2026/); [Expat NL Hub](https://expatnetherlandshub.com/guides/finance/best-finance-apps-expats-netherlands-2026/)
- Incumbents are actively targeting the same 18-25 group, for example ING's Spaartaxi campaign in Sep 2025 — [InFinance](https://infinance.nl/geldzaken/sparen/ing-zet-spaartaxi-in-om-jongeren-te-leren-sparen/)
- Gen Z PFM users are seen as valuable to acquirers: RISK bought Dyme for its young users — [Quote](https://www.quotenet.nl/zakelijk/a66036154/dyme-joran-iedema-amsterdam-risk-verzekeringen-fintech-insurtech/)

### Inferences
- **Closest competitors, by dimension:**
  - Manual envelope method: **YNAB**
  - Dutch PSD2 manual categorization: **Spendle**
  - Pots plus a social or group element: **Revolut** (Pockets and Group Pockets)
  - Budget sub-accounts plus a split ledger: **bunq** (Easy Budgeting plus Tricount)
  - Getting money back: **Tikkie**
- No found product combines all five in Dutch for 18-30s.
- **Distinctive elements to emphasize:**
  - The sorting ritual itself: swipe or one tap, a daily streak and an evening reminder. This is a behavioural product, not a feature, and bank apps are unlikely to add manual-sorting friction.
  - Repayment-aware pots: the pot shows net spending after friends pay back.
  - Multi-bank coverage: ING plus bunq plus Revolut in one view.
- **Risks:**
  - The manual effort may cause churn, so the streak and reminder must carry retention.
  - Banks could copy streaks or gamification.
  - PSD2 consent must be renewed every 90-180 days.
  - Monetization is hard because the Dutch norm is free or under EUR 25 per year.
  - Exit precedent is acquisition for the user base (Dyme to RISK), not standalone profitability.
- A possible positioning line: "YNAB-discipline, Tikkie-simplicity, in het Nederlands, voor je eerste eigen geld."

### Gaps
- I found no Dutch survey data on how many 18-30-year-olds use budgeting apps or bank budgeting features, or what share would accept manual sorting. User research would need to cover this.
- I did not collect App Store or Google Play ratings or review counts for the competitors, so user satisfaction cannot be compared quantitatively.
- I found no evidence either way that any bank plans streaks or gamified daily review.
